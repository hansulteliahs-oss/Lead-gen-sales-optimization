## 3. Program Overview

### 3.1 What Is an Au Pair

> "An au pair is a young adult (18–26) who lives with an American family and provides up to 45 hours of childcare per week as part of a cultural exchange."

- **Visa:** J-1 cultural exchange, obtained via U.S. embassy in home country
- **Regulator:** U.S. Department of State
- **Initial commitment:** Minimum one year
- **Extension options:** 6, 9, or 12 months (if in good standing and requirements met)

### 3.2 Work Rules (State-Department regulated)
- Max **10 hours/day**
- Max **45 hours/week**
- Childcare-related tasks **only**
- **No** overtime allowed, even with extra pay
- **2 weeks paid vacation/year**

### 3.3 Typical Duties
- Assist with newborns; independently care for children 3+ months old
- Morning and bedtime routines
- Kids' meals and snacks
- Children's laundry and room tidiness
- School drop-off and activity transport

### 3.4 Cost Comparison: Au Pair vs. Nanny vs. Daycare

| Option   | Monthly Cost    | Notes                              |
|----------|-----------------|------------------------------------|
| Au pair  | **$1,785**      | Per family, **not per child**      |
| Nanny    | $3,544          | Increases per child                |
| Daycare  | $788–$3,693     | Increases per child                |

**Scheduling:**
- Au pair: "Highly flexible" — 45-hour weekly schedule adapted to family needs
- Nanny: "Somewhat flexible, but typically requires a 9-5 schedule"
- Daycare: "9-5 only — inflexible after hours and drop-off/pick-up schedules"

**Training & Screening:**
- Au pair: Certified; 200+ hrs childcare experience; Au Pair Training School **mandatory**; comprehensive multi-tiered screening
- Nanny: No formal training required; "little to no screening"
- Daycare: Licensed staff; varies

**Only au pairs are "designed for cultural exchange and sharing of language and traditions."**

## 5. Host Family Requirements & Expectations

### 5.1 Hard Eligibility Requirements
- U.S. citizens or legal permanent residents
- English as primary home language
- All adults in household pass criminal background check
- Willing to embrace cultural exchange spirit

### 5.2 Must Provide
- **Private bedroom** for the au pair
- 3 meals/day
- Utilities
- Car + auto insurance (for childcare duties + au pair personal activities)
- U.S. phone + phone plan (for work-hour contact)
- Up to **$500/yr** toward 6 required college credits
- Weekly stipend of at least $195.75

### 5.3 Cultural-Exchange Expectations
- "Welcome your au pair as you would an extended family member"
- Include in family meals and activities
- Support cultural adjustment; explain household customs
- Recognize birthdays and holidays
- Maintain stipend regardless of illness/absence
- Backup childcare when au pair is ill
- Help navigate U.S. healthcare system if needed
- Invest time in meaningful relationship

### 5.4 Special Cases
- **Single parents:** "There are lots of single-parent families who host Cultural Care au pairs."
- **Special-needs children:** Possible, but matching takes longer; au pair must have required experience per State Dept. regs.
- **Known candidate:** Possible if they meet requirements + complete screening. **"An au pair cannot be placed in the home of a relative."**
- **Shared au pair:** **Not allowed** — au pairs reside with and care for only one family.

## 9. Full FAQ Content

*(Verbatim from `/frequently-asked-questions/`. Reuse directly on a `/faq` page.)*

### General

**What is an au pair?**
Programs bringing young adults aged 18-26 from overseas to live with American families on a legal visa for up to two years, providing childcare while experiencing cultural exchange and receiving room, board, and academic support.

**What does an au pair do?**
Lives with your family and helps care for children on a schedule you decide (up to 10 hours daily, max 45 hours weekly). Can assist with childcare-related tasks including laundry, meal prep, light housekeeping, and transportation to school and activities.

**Where are au pairs from?**
Au pairs come from "30+ different countries around the world in Central and South America, Europe, South Africa and Asia."

**What are the au pair requirements?**
Must be ages 18-26, complete secondary education, have childcare experience, commit to at least 12 months abroad, possess valid driver's license, submit doctor-issued health certificate, and pass criminal background check.

**Do all au pairs speak English?**
Yes. "All au pairs speak English, and their English skills range from proficient to native speaker." English proficiency is assessed during screening in home countries.

**Can au pairs drive?**
Yes. Au pairs are "generally licensed to drive in their home country and travel to the U.S. with an International Driver's Permit." Host families must provide appropriate auto insurance coverage.

**What preparation do au pairs undergo?**
Personal interview, reference check, and criminal background check. They complete a "32-hour online training and orientation program" covering child safety, development, CPR, and First Aid through American Heart Association.

**Why do au pairs join the program?**
Primary motivation is "the desire to become part of an American family and immerse themselves in American culture." Additional goals include improving English, building friendships, exploring the USA, and gaining international work experience.

**What's the difference between an au pair and nanny?**
"An au pair refers to a young person from overseas who lives with an American family to care for their children and participate in a cultural exchange." They travel on legal J-1 visa through designated agency with State Department-regulated hours. "Nanny" is broader terminology for various caregiving arrangements.

**What is a host family?**
"A host family welcomes an au pair into their home to secure live-in, reliable childcare and participate in a cultural exchange." Must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents, fluent in English, and provide private bedroom, meals, and weekly stipend.

**Should I host an au pair?**
Host if: seeking flexible, reliable caregiver; can provide private bedroom and three daily meals; parents are U.S. citizens or permanent residents; all household adults pass criminal background check.

**What is the role of the agency?**
Ensures "State Department regulations are followed" and au pair arrives with necessary visa. Conducts interviews, reference checks, document verification in recruitment countries. Provides orientation, CPR/First Aid training, and ongoing monthly contact and support throughout program term.

### Au Pair Costs

**How much does an au pair cost?**
"The estimated annual au pair program cost starts at $21,424, or about $1,785 per month." Total comprises program fee of $11,245 plus minimum weekly stipend of $195.75 (totaling $10,179 annually). Additional costs include room/board, insurance, education contributions.

**How much is the au pair weekly stipend?**
"Host families must pay their au pairs a minimum weekly stipend of $195.75," calculated using federal minimum wage and housing credit. Families may agree to higher amounts but cannot exceed 10 hours daily or 45 hours weekly limits.

**Do I have to pay the whole program fee upfront?**
No. Monthly payment plan available with non-refundable deposit of $3,749 (one-third of fee) at matching, then $670 monthly installments starting at au pair's arrival.

**Do we pay extra for having more than one child?**
No. "Host families pay the same program fee and weekly stipend regardless of how many children they have."

**Is it possible to use a dependent care FSA to pay for au pair childcare?**
Yes. "Au pairs are considered eligible expenses under the dependent Flex Spending Account (FSA)." Families may also qualify for dependent care tax credit.

### Host Family Application

**What are the host family requirements?**
Must: be U.S. citizens or permanent residents; pass criminal background check; provide private bedroom. Must embrace cultural exchange spirit through active participation and meaningful relationship building.

**How do I apply to be a host family?**
"You can apply by downloading our app or clicking the pink 'Get started' button on our website."

**How long does it take to get an au pair?**
Varies by family. Most au pairs in home countries need "at least six to eight weeks" for visa, training, and preparation. Some candidates already in the U.S. can arrive faster. Filters available to match arrival timeframes.

**As a single parent, am I able to participate in the program?**
Absolutely. "There are lots of single-parent families who host Cultural Care au pairs."

**My child has special needs. Can we still host an au pair?**
Possible, but program must accommodate child's needs and au pair must have required experience per State Department regulations. May take longer to find match, but Matching Specialist available to assist.

### Matching

**How does the matching process work?**
After profile creation, search for au pairs using app filters, favorite candidates, and initiate chat or video calls. Extend offers to matches. Matching Specialists assist throughout search process.

**What if I already know someone that I want to welcome as our au pair?**
Possible if candidate meets requirements and completes screening/interview process. Host family must still register, create profile, and interview prospective au pair. "An au pair cannot be placed in the home of a relative."

**Can two families "share" an au pair?**
No. "Two families cannot share an au pair. Au pairs are required to reside with and provide childcare for only one family during their program term."

**Do you accept male au pairs?**
Yes. "Male au pairs are some of our most successful caregivers and represent about 10 percent of the program enrollment." Often become "energetic big brother figures and positive role models."

### Hosting

**How long can an au pair stay?**
"All au pairs commit to staying with their host families in the U.S. for at least one year" with legal J-1 visa. Can extend for additional 12, 9, or 6 months if Education Requirement completed and in good standing.

**What is expected of me as a host family?**
"Embrace the cultural exchange spirit" by welcoming au pair as extended family member, explaining household rules/routines, asking about their day, remembering holidays/birthdays, including them in family meals, and supporting their exchange experience benefits.

**What do I need to provide for my au pair?**
"A suitable private bedroom, weekly stipend, up to $500 per year toward their college classes, and a supportive home environment." Must also provide transportation to classes/outings, cell phone plan, and car insurance if driving required.

**I'm worried about losing privacy. How do families handle this?**
Privacy concern "usually disappears shortly after an au pair arrives." Au pairs have private bedroom for off-duty time and spend much free time with friends, attending classes, and exploring.

**Can we get an au pair at any time during the year?**
Yes. "Cultural Care Au Pair arranges for au pairs to arrive in the U.S. almost every week out of the year."

**What type of household duties can au pairs help with?**
Can assist with "household tasks directly related to the children, such as managing their laundry, preparing meals, and keeping their rooms tidy." May contribute to shared tasks but duties must remain reasonable without heavy-duty cleaning or sole assignments.

**Can my au pair work extra hours if I pay them extra money?**
No. "U.S. Department of State regulations strictly forbid au pairs to work beyond the 10 hours per day, 45 hours per week limit, even for extra pay."

**Can my au pair care for my children overnight?**
Yes, but all on-duty hours count as working hours, including overnight. Cannot exceed 10 hours in 24-hour period. Long-term overnight care affects au pair quality of life. Speak with Matching Specialist and share overnight needs in profile.

**Can my au pair come on vacation with us?**
Yes, families welcome au pairs on vacation. Eligibility to travel outside U.S. depends on home country and visa status. Consult Local Childcare Consultant regarding required documents. Define whether au pair is on-duty or off-duty.

**What happens if it doesn't work out with my au pair?**
Contact Local Childcare Consultant or staff member. "The majority of conflicts between host families and au pairs happen as a result of personality differences or misunderstandings" and usually resolve through communication. May continue program with different au pair or discontinue with applicable refund per Host Family Agreement.

**Do au pairs have medical insurance?**
All au pairs have "travel insurance coverage provided by Erika Insurance Ltd. (Erika)" during program term. Erika partners with Aetna Student Health: **1-800-783-7447** or **erika@aetna.com**

**What do we do if there is an emergency?**
"In the event of an emergency, you can reach an on-call Cultural Care staff member, 24-hours a day, seven days a week at **1-800-333-6056**."
